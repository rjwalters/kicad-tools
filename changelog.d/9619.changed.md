- **`kct check` clearance comparisons now use KiCad's acceptance band** (Issue #9619). The *checker-side* clearance slack (`DRC_TOLERANCE` in `validate/rules/base.py` plus its reporting twin in `manufacturers/fabrication_process.py`) moves from 1e-4 mm (0.1 um) to 5e-4 mm (0.5 um) -- the largest shortfall pinned KiCad 10.0.6 accepts on the black-box probe (re-probed at 10 nm steps refined to 1 nm: a 500 nm shortfall is accepted, 501 nm flagged, on parallel tracks, a 45-degree pair and a pad-to-track pair alike; pinned in `tests/test_clearance_slack_kicad_band_9619.py`). Marginal gaps of 100-500 nm that `kct check` used to report as failing are now accepted, matching `kicad-cli drc`; shortfalls beyond 500 nm still fail. Everything that *writes* copper is deliberately unchanged at 100 nm (the new `WRITER_CLEARANCE_MARGIN_MM`): the router's commit gates, `router/io.py`, the lattice and the C++ `clearance_kernel.hpp`, `kct pcb move-via` and `kct fix-vias`' via relocations, and the placement optimiser's incremental DRC all keep their own 1e-4 margin so copper kct writes stays a margin inside KiCad's tolerance -- rounding on save cannot tip it into a `kicad-cli` violation (a documented split, not a parity bug). Creepage verdicts also keep 1e-4: the probe measured kicad-cli copper clearance, not the IEC 60664-1 creepage requirement. The band edge gains a 1e-9 mm float guard so an exactly-at-the-band gap is decided by KiCad's integer-nanometre semantics, not board-file float dust. The trade-off: on fabs whose own DRC is stricter than KiCad's, a 100-500 nm shortfall can now pass kct's clearance checks while the fab's CAM would reject it -- KiCad parity was the explicit goal (#9619), and the opt-in `physical_copper_gap` fab-view pass still flags sub-floor gaps against the fab's own table.
